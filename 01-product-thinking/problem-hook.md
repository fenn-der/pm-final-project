# Problem Hook & Value Proposition (Module 1)

## Responses
Chosen path, which scenario are you committing to? (StreamLine or RouteLogic, or your own):
- ** RouteLogic
**Strategic crisis, based on the brief, if the company does nothing for 12 months, what happens to its market position?:
- ** If we do nothing for 12 months, we are at risk of losing our largest account to a leaner competitor. That account is already calling our product an “administrative burden” and the actual users of the product (the coordinators who run the daily fleet operations) are using it less. If that trial goes well then other customers might run similar tests and switch. Furthermore, if the coordinators are using alternatives in their every day, less of their work is recorded in our system, and any reporting that relies on it will become less reliable over time. A year from now we risk losing the core value of what our business is: a system the frontline runs its day on, which is the only thing that makes our reporting worth paying for. Without that, the C-suite has no real reason to renew and we risk losing accounts over time as the contracts run out and people potentially switch to our competitors.
Moment of misery, what is the user likely doing outside the product (Google, spreadsheets, a competitor) because your tool isn’t serving them?:
- ** A coordinator learns mid-morning that a customer has moved to a new address, with that day’s deliveries already on the road. The route has to be reworked at short notice. Doing that in RouteLogic takes too many steps, so the coordinator calls the driver, reworks the stops in a shared spreadsheet and sends the changes through a group chat. The delivery arrives, but the plan is no longer tracked in RouteLogic and can't feed the reporting tool directly. Later the coordinator either enters everything into RouteLogic a second time, which is the “administrative burden” our largest account describes, or does not enter it at all (worst case scenario).
Problem hook, summarize the business risk and user pain into one urgent sentence.:
- ** Coordinators are using RouteLogic less and less because it slows their most time-sensitive work, and our largest enterprise account is already trialing a leaner competitor because of it.
Value proposition, based on the proposed initiative (Spotlight or Velocity), what is the new value and why is it urgent to launch now?:
- ** For the coordinators who run daily fleet operations, RouteLogic Velocity will strip the product back so they can handle a short-notice change, like reworking a route, faster inside our tool than in a spreadsheet. Enterprise features stay available but out of their way, and because the work happens in our system again, the reporting our customers’ C-suite relies on stays complete. This is urgent because our largest enterprise account is already trialing a leaner competitor and more customers could follow suit.

## Cold-read your own hook
Is the business risk high enough to justify a new initiative, a high-stakes threat, or a minor inconvenience?:
- ** This is a high-stakes threat. Our largest enterprise account is trialing a competitor, and usage has dropped sharply among the coordinators who are our core users, so this goes beyond one unhappy customer. No revenue has been lost yet, but in a contract business lost revenue shows up last, after the customer has already decided. It needs an initiative and not a quick fix because the cause is years of saying yes to everything. That is not a bug or a UI issue that one rework can solve. It also means changing how we decide what to build, or the product will bloat again.
Is the moment of misery a systemic problem or just an edge case?:
- ** This is a systemic issue. The brief says that for the 95% who rely on the product daily, the extra layers have turned a simple job into a struggle, and usage has dropped sharply among the people responsible for real-time fleet coordination. Our largest account also says the product slows their most time-sensitive work. What I don’t know yet is what these struggles look like in practice. The route change in my example is my own illustration, and confirming how often moments like it happen is exactly what the audit of the existing user research should do.
Does the value proposition actually remove the obstacle you identified, or is it a generic feature?:
- ** The obstacle is that a short-notice change, like reworking a route, takes too many steps in RouteLogic, so coordinators do it elsewhere. The value proposition removes that only if Velocity makes that task itself shorter, and not just the screens around it tidier. That is why the promise is specific: faster inside our tool than in a spreadsheet. If we deliver that, the work stays in RouteLogic and the reporting for the C-suite stays complete. If we only hide features, it is a generic “simpler product” and the obstacle remains.
