# Strategic Discovery Map (Module 1)

## Responses
- **Write one feature from the map that, if an engineer asked “why is this a priority for our 12-week roadmap?”, you honestly could not give a strategic answer for.:** Goals are a priority for our 12 week roadmap because it introduces an element of gamification and progress to the user. Adding goals is an easy win because there are many low-effort/low-cost ways to allow the user to set goals. If we cannot keep people working towards something our retention rate will suffer and we will not remain competitive in the saturated water tracking app market.
- **Write one feature that looks “correct” for the app but has zero strategic weight, you have no idea if it helps the company make money or whether a single user actually asked for it.:** Custom cup sizes. It seems like a natural fit for a water tracking app, but nothing in the map connects it to one of the listed user needs, and those needs came from the AI, not from real users. I have no idea whether a single user asked for it or whether it helps the company make money. I assume most people are fine with standard sizes, but that is also just my guess. The map is well organized, but that does not tell me this is worth engineering time.
- **Compare V3 to your Step 1 baseline. What changed, and is the output objectively better, did the AI get smarter, or did your added PM knowledge (JTBD, effort/impact, retention) force it to be useful?:** The input changed, not the AI. It was the same tool each time. V1 is six feature groups with no user, no goal and no ranking. V3 starts from a hypothesis (win by removing effort, not adding reminders), names the riskiest assumption with a cheap way to test it, ranks features by impact and effort, and marks the tensions we would need to debate. It even states that its ratings are hypotheses, not data. The output is better because the PM knowledge in the prompt forced it to be useful.
- **Key takeaway: how does this prove the PM’s role is to think while the AI’s role is to execute?:** My key takeaway is that this does not prove that the PM’s role is only to think and the AI’s role is only to execute. The exercise does show that the output depends on the context I give it and that the decisions stay with me. V3 says “the top three rows are the MVP”, but that is a call only the team can make, and the map itself says its ratings are hypotheses. I also think part of the benefit of AI is that it challenges our thinking. V3 rated the impact of streaks and gamification as unclear and listed arbitrary goals and streak shame as risks, which questions the argument I would have made for goals in the first step. AI has moved beyond just execution and can support and challenge us through quick discovery, prototyping and iteration.
